## Hard rule: never framed as injury treatment

Mobility work is preparation and recovery, not rehabilitation. Never present a mobility routine as treating,
fixing, or preventing a specific injury — that's SR-003 territory (no prescribing rehab) regardless of what
prompted the request. If the user describes something that sounds like an injury rather than routine stiffness,
the response is [references/safety-rules.md](safety-rules.md), not a mobility routine.

## Grounding from the evidence

- **Warm-up:** improves performance in the large majority of studies reviewed, via warmer muscle and a primed
  nervous system — real and worth doing, especially before faster efforts. The RAMP structure (raise,
  activate/mobilise, potentiate) is the source's own recommended shape; full RAMP before quality/harder efforts,
  an abbreviated raise-plus-a-little-mobility version for easy runs. *(moderate; warm-up-and-cool-down)*
- **Cool-down:** near-universal but does little — doesn't measurably speed recovery of soreness, muscle-damage
  markers, stiffness, or range of motion, and doesn't prevent injury, though it doesn't hurt training adaptation
  either. Present it as optional comfort ("do it if it feels good"), never as a recovery necessity.
  *(the same page grades this half weak, distinct from its own moderate warm-up half; see
  warm-up-and-cool-down)*
- **Static stretching doesn't prevent overuse injury:** this is independently established at `coaching-principles.md`'s
  own moderate grade (the strength-training-vs-injury meta-analysis found strength training roughly halves
  injury risk while stretching showed no such protective effect) — so this conclusion is used here despite also
  appearing on the contested-graded `stretching.md` page, because it doesn't rest on that page alone.
  *(moderate; via coaching-principles.md's "Pain and injury pattern recognition")*
- **Long static holds before running can briefly reduce force output** (holds of 60+ seconds per muscle showed
  the largest effect in a multilevel meta-analysis; shorter holds, much less so) — another reason a dynamic
  warm-up is preferred to static stretching before running, separate from the injury-prevention question above.
  This specific claim sits on the contested-graded page alone, so it's noted here as directional context, not a
  firm rule the coach should assert as settled. *(contested; stretching)*
- **More flexibility isn't better** — static flexibility is unrelated or slightly negatively related to running
  economy, since some muscle-tendon stiffness helps store and return elastic energy. Don't frame greater
  flexibility as a training goal. *(contested; stretching — noted for completeness, not asserted as settled)*
- Foam rolling specifically is also not shown to reduce injury risk (see `coaching-principles.md`'s "Pain and
  injury pattern recognition") — same framing as static stretching: comfort tool, not an injury-prevention
  lever.

## Areas covered (FR-011)

Ankles, calves, hips, glutes, hamstrings, thoracic (upper back/rotation), general pre-run movement preparation,
and gentle post-run/recovery movement.

## Delivery formats

- **Pre-run** (2-5 minutes): dynamic, movement-based — leg swings, ankle circles, walking lunges with a
  rotation, hip openers. Never static holds before running.
- **Post-run** (optional, 3-8 minutes): gentler, can include static holds if the user wants them (comfort, not
  a requirement) — calves, hips, hamstrings.
- **Standalone** (5-15 minutes, on a non-running or recovery day): the most room for thoracic rotation work,
  longer static holds, and anything the user specifically wants to work on.

## Default rotation by area

- **Ankles:** ankle circles, calf-wall stretch or standing calf raise through full range, walking on
  toes/heels briefly.
- **Calves:** standing calf stretch (straight and bent knee to hit both muscle heads), light bouncing/pogo taps
  if used as pre-run activation.
- **Hips:** walking lunge with rotation, leg swings (front-back and side-to-side), 90/90 hip switches or
  standing hip circles.
- **Glutes:** glute bridges (can double as light activation pre-run), clamshells, standing hip abduction.
- **Hamstrings:** dynamic leg swings pre-run; standing or seated hamstring stretch post-run/standalone only.
- **Thoracic:** open-book rotation, standing trunk rotations, cat-cow — best as a standalone or post-run
  inclusion, not usually pre-run.
- **General pre-run prep:** a short raise-activate-potentiate sequence (brisk walk/light jog, dynamic drills
  above, a few strides) rather than isolated stretching.

## Adaptation rules

- **By run type:** long runs and quality/controlled-quality sessions get the fuller pre-run RAMP sequence;
  easy/recovery runs get an abbreviated version (raise + one or two dynamic movements is enough).
- **By volume:** in higher-volume weeks, keep standalone sessions short and prioritize the areas most loaded by
  running (ankles, calves, hips) over general full-body mobility.
- **By reported stiffness:** if a check-in or conversation flags stiffness in a specific area, add extra
  standalone time for that area next session — framed as "extra attention," never as treating an injury (if it
  sounds like pain rather than stiffness, apply safety-rules.md instead).
- **By `mobility_preferences`:** if the profile states a preference (e.g., "already does yoga," "prefers short
  routines," "dislikes floor work"), adapt the format and duration accordingly rather than issuing a generic
  routine.
- **By available time:** every routine above should have a "short version" (pick 2-3 of the listed movements)
  for when the user has under 5 minutes.
